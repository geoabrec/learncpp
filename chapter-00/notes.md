## 0.1 – Introduction to these tutorials

**In my own words:** The main purpose of learncpp.com is to help you learn the proper guidelines and best practices
for programming in C++ while keeping it engaging, current, and free to use. There are habits
that will ensure you stay on track: writing the examples by hand, always debugging whenever
you encounter an error, experimenting with the example programs (changing them a bit), not rushing the quizzes and
taking your time with them, and writing simple programs yourself from what you've learned. The main goal is to practice
by writing real programs. They start small but grow as you learn.

**Key concepts/terms:**
- Debugging: Finding and fixing errors, or bugs, in your program.
We can divide them, roughly, in three types: syntax errors, runtime errors, and logical errors.

    - Syntax errors: Mistakes or typos that break the rules of the programming language. These happen at compile time.
    For example, forgetting to finish a statement with a semicolon (;). These are usually simple
    to catch and fix, as your compiler will refuse to build your program, telling you which line the error is on,
    and your text editor will usually give you a hint also.

    - Runtime errors: Errors that make the program crash, or not finish properly, when you execute it.
    For example, dividing an integer by 0.

    - Logical errors: Unexpected behaviour from the program you are writing. The program runs, but doesn't
    do what you intended. These errors often require you to know what the program is supposed to do,
    and make you define what "correct" means for your program.
